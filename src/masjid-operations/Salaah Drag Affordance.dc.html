A · Groove Depth

Each settable time sits in a recessed channel with a thumb at its edge. A value in a groove reads as a value that slides.

Why it could win. Spends no colour at all, so it is immune to the contrast rule that killed the coloured pills. Reads as physical.

Where it fails. Quiet on a dense card, and a recessed field can read as disabled rather than draggable.

B · Grip dots Icon convention

The material drag-handle dot grid sits immediately in front of each settable value, inside its own column.

Why it could win. The most widely understood grab-me mark there is, and monochrome, so contrast is a non-issue.

Where it fails. Two dot grids per row is a lot of texture beside four times; it can read as clutter.

C · Field chip Form convention

Each settable time wrapped in a bordered chip with the chevrons inside, so it reads as a control rather than printed text.

Why it could win. People touch things that look like inputs unprompted, and it makes settable-versus-calculated obvious at a glance.

Where it fails. Says editable clearly but not draggable specifically; a first-timer may tap and wait for a keyboard.

D · Split zones Spatial

A hairline splits the card into its two halves, and a 2px rail underlines only the settable value in each half.

Why it could win. Teaches the actual rule — left half moves the azaan, right half changes the delay — instead of decorating two values.

Where it fails. The smallest affordance of the four; it may not survive the would-a-committee-member-notice test.
